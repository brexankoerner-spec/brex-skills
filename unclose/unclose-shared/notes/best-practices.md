## How to use this document

This is the reference knowledge base for the unclose coaching skill. Each section covers one submission type: what strong responses look like, what weak ones look like, the best follow-up moves, and how to handle near-cancellation situations. The Universal Principles section at the end applies to every type.

When coaching a response, match the thread's submission type to the section below, apply the relevant principles, and layer in the universal principles on top.

## UNRESPONSIVE

**What it is:** Customer stopped responding after KO (or never responded). 10+ outreach attempts, 20+ days since last interaction.

### Strong opening (in the bot submission fields)
- Document the outreach log with *specific dates*: calls, texts, voicemails, no-shows, and the moment they stopped responding.
- State the porting or setup blocker if there is one — unresponsiveness often has an underlying cause, not just radio silence.
- Name the AE and make a specific ask: "Requesting AE attempts direct contact before next week."
- Tone: factual, matter-of-fact. Not apologetic. Not frantic. You've done the work — document it.

**Model structure:** situation → dated outreach log → current status → specific ask of AE

**Strong example:** Andrew Fox (Mesa Revenue Partners) — logged every outreach attempt with dates, showed multiple channels (call, text, voicemail, email), and documented the customer's behavior (hanging up). The submission did the arguing for him.

### Weak opening patterns
- Listing attempts without dates: "I have called multiple times" tells management nothing.
- No ask of the AE: leaving it unclear who owns next steps.
- Reactive rather than documented: "I've been trying but can't reach them" without specifics.
- Waiting for management to ask for an update before posting one.

### Best follow-up moves
1. **Loop in the AE with a specific ask the same day the submission posts** — don't wait to see if management redirects you.
2. **Set a deadline in the thread** (or invite management to): "If no customer contact by [date], I recommend proceeding with unclose." This pre-empts the thread going dormant.
3. **Update the thread after every outreach attempt**, even if the update is "called again, left voicemail, next attempt tomorrow." Silence looks like inaction.
4. **When a meeting is booked**, announce it immediately and post the outcome immediately after.

**Model resolution message:** "Got [customer] on a call and completed most of the setup. We have sent the WC code to his admin and also sent a help article on how to connect his GMB page. I have a check-in meeting on [date] to make sure everything got connected." — Andrew Fox (Mesa Revenue Partners)

### Ownership vs. uncertainty language
| Strong | Weak |
|---|---|
| "I texted her and Ally and never heard back — here's the log" | "No luck for me!" |
| "Got [customer] on a call and completed most of the setup" | "Haven't been able to get an answer yet but I'll keep trying" |
| "If no response by Friday, I will flag for management" | "I'll keep trying" |

### Near-cancellation handling
Unresponsive threads usually aren't about a customer who wants to leave — they're about a customer who has gone quiet. Avoid framing the situation as cancellation risk; frame it as a logistics and access problem. When the product itself is partly the issue (e.g., porting failure), explore a partial save (phones-only unclose, rest of platform retained) before recommending a full unclose.

### Resolution patterns
- Saves usually come from AE direct engagement after OM escalation, not from OM outreach alone.
- The OM's job is to create the conditions (documented outreach, clear escalation) for the AE to step in effectively.
- Threads that stalled had one pattern: OM going reactive, waiting for management to chase updates.

## UNFIT BUSINESS

**What it is:** Business is not ready to partner — common reasons include: no tech skills, ownership change, franchise constraints, family emergency, no GMB/website, business not operational.

### Strong opening
- State specifically what's making the business unfit — not just "they're struggling" but what specifically is the blocker.
- Document what you've already attempted to bridge the gap (calls, walkthroughs, alternative setup paths).
- Include emotional/contextual detail if relevant — it helps management calibrate patience vs. urgency.
- End with a specific ask: "Requesting AE joins next call to reset expectations" is better than leaving it open.

**Model structure:** what the customer is struggling with → what OM has already tried → what specifically the OM needs from the AE or management

### Weak opening patterns
- Stating doubt without direction: "I am not sure if we will fully be able to set them up" signals the OM has mentally given up, even if they haven't said it.
- Listing what happened without proposing what should happen next.
- Making the submission a status update rather than an action request.

**Weak phrase to avoid:** "They may not be able to utilize this technical platform." This is a conclusion that belongs in the final unclose request, not the opening submission.

### Best follow-up moves
1. **Bring the AE in with prep work done**: when looping in the AE, send them a bulleted list of what the customer needs to bring to the next call (logins, account info, etc.) so the call is productive immediately.
2. **Report incremental progress** — "we got them about 60% of setup completed today" keeps management informed and motivated without overpromising.
3. **Frame emotional/relational context for the AE** — knowing "customer had a health emergency" vs. "customer is technically overwhelmed" changes the approach.
4. **When the customer seems like a capability fit but is struggling**, explore whether a simpler onboarding path (fewer products, cover OM assist, longer timeline) is possible before recommending unclose.

### Ownership vs. uncertainty language
| Strong | Weak |
|---|---|
| "I have spent 2 hours between their KO and follow-up attempting to set up their account" | "I am not sure if we will fully be able to set them up" |
| "I looped in [AE] to see if we could expedite a single desk phone" | "They ignored me during that portion of the call" |
| "Teamwork here really helped the customer get back on track" | "They may not be able to utilize this technical platform" |

### Near-cancellation handling
Reframe "they can't do this" as a capability problem the right support can solve, not a compatibility mismatch. Neither the OM nor the AE should say "this customer can't do this" in the thread until all support paths have been tried. The framing is: the customer needs more support, not that the customer is wrong for the product.

When it genuinely isn't working out, surface it factually: "Customer has missed three setups, has not been able to complete any configuration independently, and has stated they feel overwhelmed. I believe additional support would not change the outcome." That's a recommendation based on evidence — not defeatism.

### Resolution patterns
- Saves come from sustained multi-party collaboration (OM + AE + cover OM or CSM).
- The strongest saves involved an AE who treated the customer call as a fresh start, not a rescue.
- Unfit Business unclosings usually happen when the business has a structural problem (ownership change, franchise exit, closed) rather than a capability problem — capability problems are generally solvable.

## CANCELLATION REQUEST

**What it is:** Customer formally requested cancellation, typically within 3 business days of KO or C/W, or within 30 days with no KO completed.

### Strong opening
- State the cancellation request factually, including the customer's exact reason.
- If there are multiple factors (e.g., timing + product gap), list both — this gives management a full picture.
- Note whether the customer left a door open ("reconsidering in April" vs. "I'm done").
- Document what you've done to understand or address the objection.

**Model structure:** what the customer said → OM's interpretation of the root cause → whether the door is open → proposed path forward

### Weak opening patterns
- Asking management "what are next steps?" or "what do I do now?" in the thread — procedural questions the OM should know or research independently.
- Soft-pedaling a firm cancellation as a maybe: if the customer is done, say so. If they left a door open, say that.
- Not following up when the customer said "call me in X weeks" — that calendar check-in is the OM's job, not management's.

### Best follow-up moves
1. **Update the thread proactively when circumstances change** — if the customer changes their mind, re-engages, or sets a date, post it immediately without being asked.
2. **When there's a partner/program complication** (Trane, MAX, NMG), loop in the specialist early — don't try to figure out the rules yourself in the thread.
3. **When the customer says "later"**, establish specifically what "later" means and put a proposed re-engagement date in the thread.
4. **Identify what's procedurally blocking the re-sign or amendment** and route it to the right person with a specific ask.

### Ownership vs. uncertainty language
| Strong | Weak |
|---|---|
| "Good news! The customer just emailed me saying she is ready to set up now!" | "What are next steps for that?" |
| "I tried asking them more about why, and tried making a plan with them" | "I'm not sure of the next steps. What needs to be done?" |
| "Customer confirmed they want to cancel — their reason is [specific]" | "I reached out but he basically declined" (no proposed next action) |

### Near-cancellation handling
Cancellation Request threads are different from the other types: the customer has already decided. The OM's job is not to argue — it's to understand why, leave the door open if there's a real path forward, and document accurately. Sonya Ltd is the model: OM acknowledged the request, gathered information, probed whether April was real — and let the customer come back on their own. No pressure, no spin.

What to avoid: pushing the customer for a re-sign when they're not ready, or framing the situation as more hopeful than it is to delay a clean close.

### Resolution patterns
- Saves in this category are uncommon but happen — usually when the customer's reason was timing or confusion, not genuine dissatisfaction.
- Non-saves should be clean: document the customer's reason, confirm the unclose, and move on. Don't leave threads open waiting for a customer who won't re-engage.
- Procedural complexity (re-sign flow, partner rules) is the biggest source of delays — knowing the process before you need it saves multiple days.

## PRODUCT FIT

**What it is:** Product doesn't fit a core customer need — typically phones (can't port, no CWB with DMS), AI limitations, integration gaps, or feature release timelines.

### Strong opening
- State the specific product limitation clearly — don't use generic "product fit" language.
- Confirm whether you've already verified the limitation (with integrations team, product, etc.) — submissions that include "I confirmed with [team] that this is not solvable" carry more weight.
- State what partial save you're attempting or proposing (phones-only unclose, workaround, discount path).

**Model structure:** specific limitation → verification of whether it's solvable → partial save being attempted → request for approval if needed

### Weak opening patterns
- Submitting before verifying: "I'm not sure if we have any other workarounds" should be resolved before the submission, not in the thread.
- Leaning on management to script what to tell the customer: "Any update I can give the customer?" puts the OM's communication responsibility on management.
- Preemptive defeatism from the AE ("this one will be tough to save") — OM should not echo or defer to this framing.

### Best follow-up moves
1. **Call the customer before the thread goes stale** — even after the unclose is approved, a direct call can reveal whether the customer has changed their mind or would accept a partial solution.
2. **Commit clearly when the limitation is confirmed unsolvable**: "I move to continue with the unclose process" after exhausting options is clean and professional.
3. **Explore partial saves proactively**: phones-only unclose while retaining the rest of the platform is often the right outcome and can be proposed in the submission, not left for management to suggest.
4. **Give the customer a clear holding message** when waiting on an internal review — don't go silent with the customer while the thread is open.

**Model save:** Samira Feldmann (Fatima's Fine Jewelry) — after the unclose was approved, she called the customer, re-earned trust, and reversed the unclose entirely. An approved unclose is not final.

### Ownership vs. uncertainty language
| Strong | Weak |
|---|---|
| "I was able to gain the customer's trust back over the last 2 weeks and he is willing to work with us" | "Any update I can give the customer? He is asking for verification" |
| "I move to continue with the unclose process" | "Lebish is hounding me — what updates should I give?" |
| "Please pause on the unclose for now" | "This one is a bit frustrating... I think it would be tough to truly save this one" |

### Near-cancellation handling
Product Fit threads typically have a clear customer condition: fix X or I cancel. The strong path is:
1. Verify whether X is truly unsolvable (with integrations, product, etc.)
2. If unsolvable, explore whether a partial save is possible
3. Never frame unclose as the only option until you've exhausted (1) and (2)
4. Keep the customer informed with a holding message while internal review is happening

An approved unclose is not the end — Samira Feldmann is the proof.

### Resolution patterns
- Full saves are possible even after unclose approval — requires a direct customer call after approval.
- Partial saves (phones-only unclose, rest retained) are the most common positive outcome.
- Threads that went to full unclose often had a defeatist AE and an OM who didn't push back or escalate.

## MISSET

**What it is:** Something was misrepresented or misconfigured — billing corrections, contract terms, integration capability, product availability. Often the AE oversold or the customer's expectations didn't match what was delivered.

### Strong opening
- State precisely what was misset and what evidence you have (call transcript, email, integration test results).
- Document what you've already tested or confirmed — "I have collaborated with integrations to figure out options" carries more weight than "I'm not sure if there's a workaround."
- In the "Recommended Solution to Save" field: propose a specific remedy — a discount, a workaround, an amendment. Don't write "open to any ideas."

**Model structure:** what was misset → what the customer expected vs. what they got → what's been tested/verified → specific recommended remedy

### Weak opening patterns
- Leaving the "Recommended Solution to Save" field open-ended or blank — this is your recommendation to management, not an invitation for them to do your thinking.
- Stating "I'm not sure if we have any other workarounds" — this should be resolved before submission, or framed as "I've tested X, Y, and Z; none have solved it; I believe there is no workaround."
- Not surfacing AE counter-narratives proactively (if the AE is disputing the Misset, document that in the thread before management asks).

### Best follow-up moves
1. **Surface evidence early in the thread** — screenshots of call transcripts, integration test results, email chains. Evidence pre-empts AE counter-narratives.
2. **Continue testing in the background while the thread is open** and post updates: "I have continued testing social DMs — there will not be a workaround."
3. **When asked for your recommendation**, give it directly: "I think we are good to reject these" is a clean, confident close. Don't hedge.
4. **For billing/contract Missets**: confirm whether an amendment is the right process before pushing an unclose through — unclosing and re-closing is slower and messier than an amendment.

### Ownership vs. uncertainty language
| Strong | Weak |
|---|---|
| "I have continued doing some testing — there will not be a workaround" | "Open to any other ideas with a workaround here" |
| "I have collaborated with integrations to figure out options" | "I'm not sure if we have any other workarounds" |
| "I think we are good to reject these" | [No recommendation stated] |

### Near-cancellation handling
Misset customers are usually not asking to cancel — they're asking for compensation or correction. The risk is that the situation escalates to cancellation if resolution is too slow. The OM's job is to propose a fair remedy early, verify it's the right level (not under- or over-compensating), and move fast. If the remedy available (discount, feature workaround) isn't enough to retain the customer, say so with evidence rather than hoping it holds.

### Resolution patterns
- Most Missets resolve with the unclose rejected: either an amendment is the right path, or the customer accepts the limitation with a small remedy.
- Threads that dragged involved AE pushback on the Misset classification — surface this conflict early with evidence rather than letting it slow down management review.

## DELINQUENT

**What it is:** Customer has missed their first or second invoice during onboarding. Finance/billing triggered the submission, not the OM. These are billing logistics problems, not relationship problems.

### Strong opening (for the AE — OM is rarely the driver here)
- AE should respond the same day the submission posts with context management doesn't have: why the invoice was missed, current customer relationship status, and what action the AE has already taken.
- Frame it as a logistics issue if that's what it is: "customer is live and engaged, this is a payment issue, not a relationship issue."
- State what you've already done and commit to a specific next step with a timeline.

**Strong example:** Timothy Pennington (Horman's HVAC) — "I have talked with him today and sent him a link to update billing info. He said he will get it done tonight. If not, I will call him again tomorrow."

### Weak patterns
- AE goes absent while finance/billing drives the thread — the AE has the relationship, which means the AE has the leverage. Leaving collections to finance alone is slower and less effective.
- First reply is a question ("I'm assuming it's because of NMG billing?") — show you've investigated before asking.
- Going passive after the initial outreach push: "I had assumed it was updated when I had not heard anything" — don't assume, confirm.

### Best follow-up moves
1. **AE calls the customer the same day** — don't wait for finance to chase.
2. **Use all channels if phone doesn't work** — text via Podium, send a pay request through the app, have finance try a different contact.
3. **Align on a deadline** and work against it — "If not paid by [date], unclose proceeds."
4. **Finance team as the bridge**: if the AE can't reach the customer, route to the finance rep (Caleb Leonard / Rogan Millar) who can call directly and send a payment link.
5. **Confirm payment in writing in the thread** — "I show that these guys paid on [date]" closes the loop cleanly.

### Ownership vs. uncertainty language
| Strong | Weak |
|---|---|
| "I have talked with him today and sent him a link... If not, I will call him again tomorrow" | "I'm assuming it because of NMG billing?" |
| "I show that these guys paid on [date]" | "I had assumed it was updated when I had not heard anything" |
| "This customer is live and engaged — this is a billing logistics issue" | [Radio silence from AE while finance handles it] |

### Near-cancellation handling
Delinquent submissions are billing problems, not cancellation situations — treat them that way. The default frame is: "customer is live, happy, and a payment is overdue." Keeping that frame prevents the thread from trending toward unclose approval prematurely. If the customer IS unhappy or unresponsive, surface that separately — don't conflate it with the billing problem.

### Resolution patterns
- Both sampled threads resolved with payment received and unclose rejected.
- AE + finance rep working in tandem with a clear deadline is the consistent winning pattern.
- Deadlines set by management (Sam Skanchy, John Palfreyman) were the forcing function — OMs/AEs should pre-empt this by proposing a deadline in their first reply.

## UNIVERSAL PRINCIPLES

These apply across all submission types. A coaching response should check each response against all seven.

### 1. The bot submission IS your opening response — treat it accordingly
Management reads the submission before the thread. The "Additional Details," "What has been done so far," and "Recommended Solution to Save" fields are your first impression. Vague fields ("NA," "Open to any ideas") signal the OM hasn't thought the situation through. Strong submissions include: a specific outreach log with dates, a factual diagnosis of why the account is at risk, and a concrete proposed path forward.

### 2. Own the plan, not just the activity
Weak replies document effort without direction: "I'll keep trying," "No luck for me!," "Haven't heard back." Strong replies state both the action and the intended outcome: "I texted him and called him today — I will let you know if I hear anything back." The difference between "I called him" and "I called him, here's what happened, and here's what I'm doing next" is the difference between logging work and owning a result.

### 3. Use the AE as a resource, not a last resort
OMs who waited until the thread went stale to loop in the AE created longer timelines. The strongest threads had the OM pinging the AE immediately with a specific ask (prep the customer, provide a contact, make a direct call). Specific asks outperform general requests: "Could you call Laurie today?" is better than "could you help coordinate a time?"

### 4. Set deadlines or they won't exist
In most threads, momentum stalled until management put a deadline in the thread. Strong OMs pre-empt this: "If I don't hear back by Friday, I'll flag for management." This reduces management's supervisory load and signals self-direction.

### 5. Update the thread before you're asked
Every stalled thread had the same pattern: management or the OMM asking "any update?" The OM should be posting updates proactively — even if the update is "no change yet, next outreach is tomorrow." Silence creates the impression that nothing is happening.

### 6. Don't advocate for cancellation — advocate for clarity
The strongest behavior when an account looks unsaveable is to name the reality without pushing for the unclose. "I don't think phones are saveable in this situation" is appropriate after exhausting options — *after* the AE has been looped in, the customer has been called, and management has weighed in. Preemptive defeatism (before trying everything) makes the OM look like they're giving up, not recommending. Frame assessments as evidence-based recommendations, not conclusions.

### 7. Write for the thread record, not just the moment

Every reply is part of a record that Robert Potts (final approver) reads when the thread reaches him for unclose approval. He doesn't read every message in real time — he reads the full thread at the decision point. When he does, he's trying to answer 5 questions:

1. What went wrong?
2. Why didn't we fix it quickly enough?
3. What blockers are preventing us from fixing it now?
4. Why are we willing to give up on the account?
5. What do we need to do to prevent this from happening again?

No single reply needs to answer all 5. But the thread as a whole should answer all of them by the time it reaches Potts. The practical implication: don't leave things that matter inside your head or in a separate DM. If there's a blocker, name it in the thread. If there's a reason the fix took time, document it. If the account is being let go, the record should show why — not just state it.

Potts will also opine intermittently on high-profile accounts (large deal size, significant product gaps). On those threads, write with the awareness that he may be reading before the approval stage.

### 8. Know your process before you need it
Several threads got stuck when the OM or AE didn't know what came next: how to re-sign a fully unclosed customer, when to use an amendment instead of an unclose, what a billing switchover requires, how Trane/MAX partner rules work. Procedural gaps create unnecessary delays and make the OM look less credible. Understanding the downstream process is part of professional readiness for this role.
