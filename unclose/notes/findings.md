# Research Findings — Unclose Coaching Skill

*Compiled: 2026-05-19 during planning session*

---

## Channel structure

- **Channel:** `#unclose-at-risk` (ID: `C07TZG7K7DZ`)
- **Message rate:** ~2 submission notifications/day (includes collections alerts and full unclose submissions)
- **90-day window cost estimate:** ~$0.50–$1.00 total (one-time), including thread reads
- **Per-use cost:** ~$0.01–$0.02

---

## Submission types identified

Four distinct types observed, each requiring a different OM response posture:

1. **Product Fit** — Customer has a product gap or feature mismatch. Often involves product team, AE, and escalated conversations. OM's job: drive toward a workaround, credit, or timeline commitment. Do not recommend unclose unless the gap is confirmed unfixable.

2. **Unfit Business** — Customer doesn't meet onboarding criteria (no GMB, no website, not ready). Often resolvable. OM's job: identify what needs to happen to make them fit and drive action toward that goal.

3. **Unresponsive** — Customer has gone dark. OM's job: demonstrate thorough outreach attempts and escalate to AE when needed. Show persistence, not resignation.

4. **Delinquent Billing / Collections** — Customer is past due. Different tone — more procedural. OM's job: coordinate with AE and collections rep to get the customer back in good standing.

---

## Thread dynamics

- Thread reply counts range from 2 to 100+ replies
- Complex cases (Product Fit with product team involvement, escalated customers) tend to run longest
- Threads span days to weeks — sometimes months
- Most threads involve: OM updates → manager direction → AE context → resolution or escalation

---

## Response quality patterns (preliminary)

From sampling threads, strong OM responses tend to:
- Open with a clear statement of the current situation and what is being done (not what is unknown)
- Name a specific next action with a timeline ("I'll connect with the AE by EOD Thursday")
- Loop in relevant parties proactively rather than waiting to be directed
- Keep replies short — one to three sentences, unless context genuinely requires more

Weak OM responses tend to:
- Lead with uncertainty ("I'm not sure..." / "Not sure what the next step is...")
- Provide passive status updates that require management to direct the next move
- Over-explain the problem without naming a path forward
- End without a clear commitment

## Phase 1 additions (2026-05-19)

### Full submission type list (confirmed)

6 types confirmed from 591 submissions (Feb 18 – May 19, 2026):

1. **Product Fit** (150 submissions, 25%) — Feature/integration gaps, phones not porting, AI limitations, bulk message mismatches
2. **Misset** (155 submissions, 26%) — Billing corrections, contract misreps, integration capability/pricing changes, product availability mismatches. The largest category and closely related to Product Fit — key distinction: Misset implies something was oversold or misconfigured, while Product Fit is a genuine capability limitation.
3. **Cancellation Request** (92 submissions, 16%) — Formal cancel within 3 days of KO, within 30 days without KO, new owner requests, or delinquent + cancel combined
4. **Unfit Business** (86 submissions, 15%) — Business not ready: no GMB/website, ownership change, franchise constraints, operator overwhelm
5. **Unresponsive** (76 submissions, 13%) — 20+ days, 10+ outreach attempts, post-KO or post-C/W
6. **Delinquent** (17 submissions, 3%) — Finance-triggered, missed first or second invoice

### Hybrid types observed
- Some accounts submitted under multiple types simultaneously (e.g., "Delinquent + requested cancel" is a named sub-type)
- "Misset" and "Product Fit" overlap significantly — the distinction is intent (sold incorrectly vs. genuine product gap)
- No entirely new categories found outside the 6 above

### Reviewer change confirmed
- Feb–Mar 2026 threads reference "Than and Chance" as named reviewers in the bot notification template
- May 2026 Misset/Product Fit threads: John Palfreyman and Sam Skanchy are the active approvers
- May 2026 Delinquent threads: Than Hancock still active
- Routing may have changed by submission type — treat current thread participants as the active reviewers, not the template language

### Automated alerts vs. submissions
- 283 of 874 messages in the 90-day window are automated "At-Risk Alert" billing notifications — not formal unclose submissions
- These follow a different pattern and should not be confused with submission threads
